# Dial

## Philosophy
A lifecycle is a **loop**, not a line, and what the operator needs first is the SHAPE of
his enforcement: how much of the loop is armed, and where it is open.

## How it stays readable at scale
Eleven steps are eleven equal arcs of one crown read clockwise from 12 o'clock, so a
step's angular position IS its position in the sequence. The arc's stroke is its binding
state on the shared ladder's two colourless axes, carried into SVG: a closed stroke means
the binding exists, an open one that it does not, and 9px is a state you can act on
against 4px for one you only read. Coverage is a shape before it is a number, and the hub
states the denominator where the eye already is: `enforced/total`, counting `live` only,
since the product's phrase for `detected` is "only detected, not installed". A wide gap
at each lane boundary makes the before/after split a shape. Captions are horizontal HTML
at a fixed radius, never rotated and never below the reading tier, so the font-size
setting still reaches them; the geometry is a tested pure module. The reading column sits
beside the dial, so selecting never moves the figure.

## The wow moment
The crown. Five solid teeth, two dotted, two thin dashes, closing on itself at the top.
A stranger reads "most of this is not actually enforced" in three seconds, from a shape.

## Known limits
Under `lg` the reading column stacks beneath the 352px figure and the page gets taller
than any other concept's. Evidence does not reach the ring at all, so "how often did this
hold" lives only in the ledger. Past about 16 steps the captions would collide, and the
model does not yet stagger them.
