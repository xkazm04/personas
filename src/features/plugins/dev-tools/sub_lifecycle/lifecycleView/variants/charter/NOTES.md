# Charter

## Philosophy
A project's practice is a **document an agent is handed**, so the first thing the view
owes the operator is the chance to read it: every clause, in order, with what enforces
it written in its own margin.

## How it stays readable at scale
Eleven clauses in one column is an 1100px scroll nobody finishes, so the container is a
two-page folio: before-task clauses left, after-task right, four or five against six. Each article is a numbered clause head on the reading line,
the rule at `typo-body-lg` (a reading tier, not a density tier), and an 8.5rem margin
holding one chip per binding plus the step's evidence pulse. The clause head is one type tier whether selected or not: the rail's old
defect was that selection changed a caption's tier and made the row jump, and growing on
selection is the same bug with the other sign. Under `lg` the pages stack and each margin
slides under its own prose, so nothing is dropped at any width.

## The wow moment
The rule text, all of it, at once. Nowhere else in the app can you read what the agent is
actually told without clicking eleven times, and seeing `docs` say "update the docs and
CHANGELOG your change makes stale" beside an **Advisory** chip is the whole argument for
installing a binding, made without a single number.

## Known limits
Position is carried by an ordinal and a page, not a drawn path, so "what is behind me"
reads weaker than on the rail. Nothing on the figure shows how often a clause held; that
lives only in the ledger at the foot. A long custom rule can leave one page much taller
than the other.
