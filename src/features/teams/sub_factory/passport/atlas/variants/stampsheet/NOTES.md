# Stamp Sheet

## Philosophy
A project is one recognisable GLYPH whose own strokes are its dimensions, so you
pick it out of 102 by its shape before you read a word of its name.

## How it stays readable at scale
The stamp is a fixed-size tile in an auto-fill grid, so 102 projects fit one
screen with no scroll band, no stripes and no name column to truncate. The name
is cut where its own punctuation already cuts it (`splitLead`) into a tracked
lead and a two-line title, with no ellipsis - which is the only reason an
86-character name is legible at all. The mosaic's width is near-square and
capped at six, so a thirty-dimension lens stays a block rather than becoming a
strip. Prose never drops below the body size; the only thing that shrinks is
the drawing.

## The wow moment
The dimension cursor lights the SAME stroke in all 102 glyphs at once, so you
get a column read without a column - and because the two absent states are
drawn as paper with their own inset void glyph rather than as ink, a stamp's
inked area IS the fraction of it that anybody has measured. Each stamp states
that fraction on itself (`3/14`). The loupe above the sheet is the same
component at `l`, not a different drawing.

## Known limits
Comparing two specific projects cell-for-cell is harder than in the matrix:
there is no shared x-axis, so a difference is read as a difference in shape
rather than as a difference along a row. The mosaic's reading order is the
lens's order and has to be learned once. At a single-dimension lens the stamp
degenerates to a tile with one mark.
