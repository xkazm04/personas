# The Claim

## Philosophy
A feature is not a row; it is a claim staked on ground. So the page *is* the ground and the
roster reads second — the right order when 42% of the land is unclaimed.

## How it stays readable at scale
**Job 2 is the canvas**: 191 contexts as squares in 25 plots, each sized by its own count and
dashed when no feature enters it. A square takes the temperature of whoever claims it — five
families on the map, plus platform, tests and void. **Job 1 is the left rail**, grouped by who
is waiting, where the full ten-state vocabulary keeps its seal, its label and its score against
the threshold. One selection drives both; the deed below carries the evidence.

Absence is never failure: never-councilled is a calm dashed ring, a null overall prints NOT
MEASURED, and dimmed plots are counted on screen rather than hidden.

Type: 13 labels, 15 rail rows, 16 prose, 21 rail head, 28 titles, 34 the claim percentage, 44
the overall.

Figures instead of text: which groups it crosses becomes the plots that ignite; load-bearing a
violet pip; 38% claimed the empty squares themselves; member scores a rose, width weight, reach
score, hatched when unmeasured.

## The wow moment
Select a claim and a thread draws across the land, from its primary context through every square
it holds, while the map scrolls that claim into the band the deed leaves open.

## Known limits
The thread is measured from the DOM on select, scroll and resize; in React it belongs in one
ref-held overlay, not in render. Squares are 18 px — pointer-first; the keyboard routes through
the rail and `[` `]`.
