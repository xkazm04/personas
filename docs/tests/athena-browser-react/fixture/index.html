Three things it does well

Edge-to-edge panel

A 60 cm light bar with 144 diodes behind a diffusing lens lights the full width of a standard desk. Light falls evenly across a keyboard, a notebook and a second monitor, so your eyes are not re-adapting each time they move from one to another.

Circadian schedule

The lamp moves from 5000 K in the morning to 2700 K after sunset on its own. Cooler light in the day keeps you alert; warmer light in the evening lowers the blue share of the spectrum so the lamp does not work against your wind-down.

Steady output

The driver runs the diodes on constant current rather than dimming them by pulsing. At every brightness step the light is continuous, which matters most on long reading sessions and for anyone sensitive to flicker.

Why it matters

Most of what people call eye strain at a desk is the eye working harder than it needs to: a bright screen against a dim room, a single overhead light that throws a glare across the display, and a lamp that quietly flickers at a rate you cannot see but can still feel as fatigue by the afternoon. The usual advice is sound and cheap: match the brightness of the room to the screen, put the light beside you rather than behind you, look at something far away for twenty seconds every twenty minutes, and blink. A good lamp does not replace those habits. What it does is remove the two causes you cannot fix by habit alone, uneven light and flicker, so the rest of the advice has a chance to work.
