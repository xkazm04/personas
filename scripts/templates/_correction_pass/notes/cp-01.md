## 11:05 - PATTERN - Judgment-heavy recipes usually already have the ledger; the gap is the third part

- In this lane (feasibility verdicts, architecture verdicts, defect hunts) the durable
  record almost always exists already: a per-item verdict, a coverage state, a
  classification library. So part 2 of the enrichment is cheap.
- The part that is genuinely missing is part 3. Several recipes already say a rejection is
  recorded with a reason, and then stop. Nothing says what the next run does with it.
  Ask specifically: does the recorded rejection change the SELECTION, the THRESHOLD, or
  the CONFIDENCE next time. Naming which of those three moves is what stops the sentence
  reading like a template.

## 11:06 - PATTERN - Look for the call that fails invisibly, not the headline call

- Every recipe here has an obvious judgment (the verdict, the estimate, the finding). Its
  correction loop is often half built already, so adding to it is padding.
- The productive target is the call whose error nobody notices: the item DECLINED, the idea
  waved through as ordinary, the finding suppressed under the severity floor, the area not
  chosen. Those are the ones a human overrule is the only possible evidence about, because
  no other signal ever arrives for them.

## 11:07 - TRAP - "Realized outcome" is not the same signal as "human override"

- codebase-feasibility-analysis already records realized cost against the predicted size.
  That looked like the correction loop was closed. It is not: most ideas are never built,
  so realized cost never arrives for exactly the ideas the estimate killed. The human
  override at triage is the only feedback available on that population.
- Worth checking anywhere a recipe records an outcome-after-the-fact: does that outcome
  arrive for the items the judgment SUPPRESSED, or only for the ones it passed.

## 11:34 - NO - A recipe that already measures its own confirm rate needs nothing

- codebase-latent-defect-hunt was my only no, and not because the question failed. It files
  judgments a person confirms or declines, and it runs repeatedly. It answers yes twice.
- It is a no because all three parts are already in it: declines record why in terms
  specific enough to stop the same shape being filed, the confirmed share is carried
  forward across passes, and a category confirmed rarely is narrowed rather than filed at
  the same rate. Adding a correction sentence there would be the padding the brief warns
  about, dressed as closing a gap.
- Class to check for: if a recipe already carries an acceptance rate or a confirm rate as a
  named measure, its correction loop is probably closed. Read it before reaching for the
  keyboard.

## 11:36 - DECISION - "The world moved" is the input; the person's reaffirm is the correction

- Both decision-record recipes look at first like the world corrects them rather than a
  person: a dependency is replaced, a service retires, the record goes stale on its own.
  On that reading you would leave them alone.
- I decided that is the wrong cut. The world moving is the recipe's INPUT SIGNAL, not a
  correction of anything. The recipe's own judgment is which records that signal makes
  likely to be wrong, and a person who reaffirms a record it put forward as contradicted
  has overruled exactly that judgment. Same shape in the linking recipe: the confirmer
  rejecting a candidate link overrules the match, not the code.
- Generalisation for other lanes: when a recipe watches the world, separate what it
  OBSERVES from what it CLAIMS about what it observed. The claim is the correctable part,
  and it is usually a selection or a threshold rather than a verdict.
